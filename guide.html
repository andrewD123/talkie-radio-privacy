1. What Talkie Radio is

Talkie Radio turns phones (and Android push-to-talk radios) into a two-way radio for your team. Hold the button, talk, let go — everyone in your group hears you right away, even when their screen is off.

  • No account. A group is just a group name + password. Same name and password = same group.
  • Encrypted. Voice, texts, photos and locations are locked on your phone (AES-256) before they leave it. Nobody outside

your group can read them — not the relays, not Talkie Communications.

  • Works everywhere there is internet (Wi-Fi or mobile data), through Talkie's own server, with public backup relays.
  • Works without internet nearby — phones pass messages to each other over Bluetooth / Wi-Fi ("mesh").

2. Getting started

  1. Install Talkie Radio from Google Play (search "Talkie Radio", by Talkie Communications).
  • If you have a test copy (APK) on the phone, uninstall it first — Play can't update a test copy.
  1. Type your name (or call sign). Your group sees it.
  2. Join a group: type the group name and the group password and tap Join. Or open an invite link / scan a QR code from a

member, or use Receive group (§7).

  1. Allow the permissions the app explains before each Android prompt:
  • Microphone — to talk (only records while you talk).
  • Notifications — to hear messages and alarms when the app is closed.
  • Nearby devices — for the mesh and Bluetooth headsets.
  • Location (optional) — to share your position with your group.
  • Keep radio on when the phone sleeps (battery exemption) — important: without it, Android may stop the radio in

the background. Tap Fix on the red banner if you see it.

  1. You're on the air. The top shows Online when you're connected.

3. The Radio screen

  • Online / Connecting / Offline (top) — your connection. Tap it for details.
  • Group card — current group, how many are online, and the green padlock "Encrypted (AES-256)". The buttons send a text,

an alert tone or a photo to the whole group. The arrow switches groups.

  • Last speaker — who talked last, with buttons to call, text, alert, photo or see details for that person.
  • PUSH TO TALK — hold to talk, release to send. It shows TRANSMITTING while you talk, RECEIVING while someone else does.
  • Replay — plays the last message again. Speaker — loudspeaker on/off. Mute — stop hearing the group for now.
  • Emergency (red) — see §5.

4. Talking and hearing

  • Hold the big button (or hold the volume-down key, or a headset button) and talk; release to send. A quick tap

on volume-down still just turns the volume down; holding it for about half a second starts talking.

  • If someone is already talking when you press, your message is recorded and sent right after them ("QUEUED") —

never cut off. (An emergency never waits — §5.)

  • Messages that arrive while you talk wait and play right after.
  • Delivery: you only see something if a message didn't reach someone ("Not received · re-sending"). The app keeps

re-sending to people who were offline for up to 1 hour.

  • Voice uses a new compressed format (Opus): smaller and clearer. 4.3 phones need Android 10 or newer.
  • If you send while the phone is offline or switching networks, the message waits and retries by itself. For the

first 10 seconds you see nothing at all; then "Sending…"; only after 60 seconds "Not sent – tap to try again" (kept in History until you retry or delete it).

5. Emergency

  • Tap Emergency and confirm. Your group gets a loud alarm, your name and your location; your microphone opens for 10 s.
  • Your controls turn red and animated, with CANCEL EMERGENCY. You hear one "dee-dah"; others hear it three times at half

volume so they can still hear you.

  • While in emergency mode, anything you say is sent as emergency. It ends only when you tap CANCEL EMERGENCY, or after

30 minutes.

  • Emergencies play over everything, wake the screen and show on the lock screen.
  • Fixed: if someone talked during your emergency, your voice could stop reaching them (your music came back on).

Your audio now stays held for the whole emergency, and the red controls stay up while you hear others.

  • On push-to-talk radios with an SOS button, the SOS button can trigger Emergency (per model).

6. Texts, photos, alerts and private messages

  • Text / photo / alert tone to the whole group from the group card, or to one person from their card (private).
  • Private calls — tap the phone icon on a person: it rings, and you both talk at once.
  • Photos to someone whose app is closed: their phone shows a notification "Sent a photo" and the photo arrives when they

open the app (photos aren't stored in the background on purpose, to save the phone's memory). Your phone keeps re-sending until then. (Planned for 4.4) You'll see "waiting for them to open the app" instead of "Not received", and the photo arrives the moment they open it.

  • "Private" messages are shown only to that person, but they use the group's key — another member with a modified app could

read them. Use a separate group for truly private talk.

7. Groups

  • Several groups at once: talk on one, hear the others (Groups tab, "Hear on").

The radio card then says "Hearing N groups"; turning a group on shows "Now hearing <group>".

  • Invite a member: Groups → invite → QR code or link. Invites work for 24 hours. Anyone with the invite knows the group

password — to cut someone off, change the group password.

  • Access log: each group keeps a log of who invited whom and who joined.
  • Supervisor lock: "Lock this phone to its group" (PIN) stops a guard's phone from changing groups.
  • Send group / Receive group (over the air, no typing):
  1. On the new radios: Groups → Receive group (that's the only tap).
  2. On your phone: Groups → Send group → pick the group. Within 2 minutes the waiting radios appear in a list.
  3. Check the list (untick anyone you don't recognise) and tap Send to all (N). Each radio joins and shows a ✓.
  • Nothing is sent until you confirm. Works without internet. Locked radios refuse.
  • A radio can only be ticked once its 4-digit code is showing next to its name (the same code is big on that radio's

screen). A radio still showing "····" is greyed out and "Select all" skips it. If a radio's code changed or it disconnected before you tapped Send, the app says "Not sent to 1 radio" - check the codes and send again.

  • The access log lists every radio that was really sent the group, one line per radio (two radios with the same name

are two lines).

  • A brand-new radio that joins this way also gets "Joined … - Undo": one tap leaves the group again.
  • A phone keeps up to 12 groups. A 13th is refused ("Too many groups"): remove one first (Groups). Nothing is ever

removed for you.

  • If you cancelled an emergency while the phone had no connection, that group can't be removed (or an over-the-air join

undone) until its members were told: the app says "Group not removed yet". The notice goes out by itself when a connection is back - also after you exit and reopen the app - or the emergency ends by itself after 30 minutes.

8. Contacts, team map, history

  • Contacts — everyone you've heard from, with online status.
  • Map — where your team is (only people who chose to share location; updated about every 15 minutes, instantly in an

emergency). Map pictures come from OpenStreetMap / CARTO; your positions are drawn on your phone.

  • History — your texts, photos and voice messages. Voice messages delete themselves after 48 hours; texts and photos are

kept up to 90 days / 500 messages. Everything stays on your phone.

9. Working without internet (mesh)

  • When phones lose internet, nearby Talkie Radio phones link over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct and pass the group's

encrypted messages along — phone to phone, hop by hop. A phone that still has internet carries them on to everyone else.

  • Range phone-to-phone: roughly 50–100 m indoors, 150–200 m+ outdoors; every phone in between extends it.
  • Settings → Mesh turns it on/off. Test without internet tries it for 5 minutes.
  • The mesh needs Google Play services on the phone (all normal Android phones have it).
  • Smart mesh: while your internet is good, the phone searches for nearby phones only now and then (saves battery);

the moment the connection weakens it goes to full speed.

  • "No internet" banner: tells you when there's no internet and whether nearby phones are linked, with buttons to

turn on Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or the mesh.

  • Confirmed on real phones 2026-11-24 (Wi-Fi Direct).

10. Staying connected

  • The radio keeps receiving in the background like a radio on your belt. A quiet notification shows this: one line,

"Talkie Radio active" (or "Talkie Radio active · sharing your location"). Open it to see "On duty · receiving your groups". It makes no sound, doesn't pop up, sits with the silent notifications and shows no details on the lock screen. Its small icon stays in the status bar, unless your phone hides silent notifications there.

  • You can swipe that notification away; the radio keeps running. While you share your location it can't be swiped away,

and its "Stop sharing location" button stays on it. Some Android 13 phones don't let you swipe it away at all.

  • If the phone's microphone stops working in the middle of a talk, that message shows as "Not sent - tap to try again";

a tap sends what was recorded.

  • A headset talk with the screen off that fails the same way shows a notification "Voice message not sent - Open Talkie

Radio to retry". Tap it: the app opens at that message in History; tap the message to send it.

  • If the microphone didn't record anything at all (it failed to start), there is nothing to send: the app says "Not sent -

talk again". Hold the button and talk again.

  • That also happens if you let go of the button before the headset microphone opened (about a second): nothing was

sent, so there is nothing to retry.

  • While you have chosen Offline (Offline ▾ → Go offline), the talk button does not record: it says "You're offline.

Tap Offline ▾ → Go online to talk." Go online first.

  • Tapping a "Not sent" message pop-up retries only that one message; other failed messages stay in History until you

tap them.

  • If you go Offline while talking, that message is not sent (also not later): it shows "Not sent - You went offline

while talking"; go online and tap it to send it. While Offline, Emergency does nothing except say "Emergency not sent - You're offline": go online first.

  • If the connection changes while you talk (for example Wi-Fi hands over to mobile data, or the internet drops and only

nearby phones are left), the app says "Connection changed - This message is sent again when you finish". Keep talking: when you let go, the whole message is sent again by itself on the new connection - also an emergency message. People who already heard the start hear only the rest. Only if that second try fails too does it show "Not sent - The connection changed while you were talking"; tap it to send it again.

  • Right after you let go of the button the status line says "Sending…"; it changes to "Message sent" once the message

really went out.

  • If the microphone doesn't finish a recording properly, it shows "Not sent - The recording didn't finish properly"; tap it

to send what was recorded. If Android itself cut the microphone (another app or a phone call took it), it says "Android interrupted the microphone while you were talking" - tap it to send what was recorded.

  • If you remove a group, anything still waiting to be sent to it is dropped with it, and a private contact you had picked

in that group is un-picked: your button talks to your talk group again.

  • A message you sent to a group you are not hearing right now (Hear is off for it) still goes out, but History shows "sent ·

no receipts while not hearing this group": the phone can't see who received it until you hear that group again.

  • A photo now keeps its place: a text you send after it arrives after the photo. Big photos get a little more time.
  • Wi-Fi with no internet → mobile data: e.g. you walk out to the driveway and the phone is still on weak Wi-Fi with no

internet. The radio notices within ~5 s and moves its own traffic to mobile data; it moves back once Wi-Fi works again. Setting: "Use mobile data when Wi-Fi has no internet" (on by default). Other apps aren't affected.

  • If the radio shows Offline while the phone has internet, see Troubleshooting.

12. Privacy and security

  • No account, no phone number, no ads, no tracking. Android ID, IMEI and phone number are never read.
  • Messages are encrypted on the phone with a key made from the group name + password. Talkie's server and the backup relays

pass encrypted data only. (Planned for 4.4) Talkie's server may keep encrypted copies of re-sent messages for up to 1 hour so a phone that was offline can catch up — it still can't read them.

  • Choose a long group password and share it only with your team.
  • Full privacy policy: https://andrewd123.github.io/talkie-radio-privacy/

14. Setting up many radios at once (supervisors)

  1. Charge the radios, install Talkie Radio, type each radio's name (e.g. "Unit 7").
  2. Allow all permissions, including the battery exemption.
  3. Put them all on Receive group, then Send group → Send to all from your phone. (Before 4.3: show the QR

invite.)

  1. Turn on Lock this phone to its group with your PIN if guards shouldn't change groups.
  2. Do a quick Radio check on each.

Words we use

Talkie Radio never listens to you, your phone calls or your surroundings. It only receives messages your group sends, and the microphone records only while you hold the talk button (or during an emergency you start). That's why the app says "ready to receive" and "hear", not "listening".

16. FAQ

Do I need an account or phone number? No. Just a group name and password.

Who can hear us? Only people who know your group name and password. Messages are encrypted on the phone.

Does it work without internet? Yes, between nearby phones (mesh). With more phones spread out, it reaches further.

Does it work if the other person's screen is off? Yes for voice, emergencies, alerts and texts. Photos and late voice messages wait until they open the app (§13).

Someone shows Online but didn't get my photo — why? Online means their radio runs in the background; photos are only taken in when their app is open (§6). A clearer label and instant delivery on open are planned for 4.4.

Why did someone go Offline? Usually Android stopped the app to save battery — allow the battery exemption (§15).

How much data does it use? Very little: about 22 kbit/s while someone is talking (older 4.2 used ~128 kbit/s).

What happens if I talk during a weak connection? It waits and retries up to 60 s silently before telling you.

What if the connection changes while I'm talking (Wi-Fi to mobile data)? Keep talking. The app says "Connection changed" and sends the whole message again by itself when you let go. You only need to tap if it then says "Not sent".

Can I use a walkie-talkie style radio instead of a phone? Yes — Android push-to-talk radios with Android 10+, Google Play services and US LTE bands. The talk / SOS buttons can be mapped (Settings → Device capabilities → button tester).

Is my location always shared? Only if you turn on "Share my location with my group"; you can stop it from the notification.

How do I delete my data? Settings → Remove this device from Talkie's server; uninstall the app to delete everything on the phone. Or email sales@talkiefiber.com.

Does Talkie keep recordings? No. Voice is not stored on the server. (A paid "recording retention" option may come later.)

How many radios can be in a group? No limit today.
