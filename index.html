Talkie Radio privacy policy

Talkie Communications · Last updated: November 24, 2026

User guide and FAQ

Talkie Radio is a push-to-talk radio app from Talkie Communications for teams such as security guards. This page explains what the app does with information. In short: there is no account and no advertising; voice, texts, photos and locations are encrypted on your phone before they leave it, so nobody outside your group - including Talkie Communications - can read them.

How the app works

You join a group by typing a group name and a group password (or by opening an invite from a member). Everyone who uses the same name and password is in the same group. Your phone turns the name and password into an encryption key (AES-256) and a random-looking group address. Voice, texts, photos, alerts, locations and check-ins are encrypted on your phone with that key and passed to the other phones in the group:

  • Through Talkie's message server (radio.talkiefiber.com), operated by Talkie Communications. It only passes the encrypted data on; it cannot read it.
  • Through public backup relays (see "Relays" below) when Talkie's server can't be reached.
  • Directly between nearby phones ("mesh"): when there is no internet, nearby Talkie Radio phones link over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and pass your group's encrypted messages along to each other. Phones outside your group can't read them. A nearby phone that has internet carries them on to the rest of the group. You can switch the mesh off in Settings.

Information the app handles

  • Your name or call sign, which you type in. It is shown to the members of your group.
  • Voice messages and private calls, recorded from the microphone only while you hold the talk button (or press a talk key), during a private call, during an emergency, or during the radio check you start yourself.
  • Texts and photos you choose to send. Photos come from the camera or gallery only when you pick them.
  • Location. If you allow it, the app shares this phone's location with the members of your group: it shows you on their team map (updated about every 15 minutes) and is sent with an emergency alert so they can find you. This continues even when the app is closed or not in use, while "Share my location with my group" is on. While it is shared in the background, the radio's notification says "Talkie Radio is on · sharing your location" and has a Stop sharing location button. The app explains this and asks before Android's permission prompt. You can switch it off any time from the notification, in Settings > "Share my location with my group", or in Android's settings. The location is encrypted for your group like any other message.
  • Online status (online, offline, app version) so your group can see who is on the radio.
  • Delivery receipts: the phones that received a message tell the sender so.
  • Device registration. The first time your phone connects to Talkie's server it creates its own device key and registers it. The server keeps a device ID made from that key (it is not a hardware or phone identifier), when it was registered and last seen, and the network it connected from (the network part of the address, not the full address). This lets the service limit misuse and, later, lets a company remove a lost or stolen phone. Only if you agree (a notice in the app asks first; you can change it in Settings > "Phone registration"), the registration also includes your name in the app and the phone's make, model, Android version and app version, so your company can tell its phones apart. The app does not read or send the Android ID, IMEI, phone number or any other hardware identifier.

What is stored, and where

  • On your phone: your name, your groups and their passwords, settings, and History (messages sent and received: voice messages are deleted automatically after 48 hours; texts and photos are kept for up to 90 days or 500 messages, whichever comes first). Uninstalling the app deletes them. Android backup is switched off for this app, so they are not copied to a cloud backup.
  • On Talkie's server: only the device registration described above. The server does not keep your voice, texts, photos or locations; it passes them on encrypted and cannot read them. A registration that is not used for 60 days is deleted automatically.
  • Other phones in your group receive what you send and keep it in their own History on their phone.

Invites (QR code and text link)

Any member of a group can make an invite: a QR code or a link that contains the group name and group password, so someone can join without typing them. You choose who you show or send it to. The invite part of the link comes after the "#", which browsers never send to this website; the join page only hands it to the app on your phone. An invite stops working in the app after 24 hours, but anyone who has it knows the group password: to cut off access, change the group password. When someone makes or uses an invite, the app tells the other members of that group (who made it, who joined, and when), encrypted like any other group message, and each phone keeps that access log on the phone.

Relays

When Talkie's server can't be reached, the app uses public MQTT relays as backups: EMQX (broker.emqx.io) and HiveMQ (broker.hivemq.com); Talkie's server also forwards your group's encrypted messages to them so phones on a backup still hear everyone. Like any internet service, a relay (and Talkie's server) sees your IP address and when your phone is connected, plus the encrypted data and its random group address. It cannot decrypt the data. The public relays handle connection data under their own policies: EMQX, HiveMQ.

Maps

The Map screen loads map pictures from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org), or from CARTO when OpenStreetMap is unavailable. These services see your IP address and which map area is shown. Your location and your group members' locations are drawn on your phone; they are not sent to the map services. OpenStreetMap privacy policy, CARTO privacy policy.

Permissions

  • Microphone: to talk. Only records while you talk, in a call, in an emergency or in the radio check.
  • Location: to share your position with your group, as described above (optional). On Android 12 it is also needed by Android to find nearby phones for the mesh.
  • Notifications: to tell you about messages, calls and emergencies when the app is not on screen, and to show that location sharing is on.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth and Wi-Fi): to use a Bluetooth headset's microphone and button, and to link with nearby Talkie Radio phones for the mesh. The app explains this before Android asks.
  • Run in the background, start after a restart, and ignore battery optimisation: so the radio keeps receiving while the screen is off, like a two-way radio. The app asks for the battery exemption only after explaining why.
  • Full-screen alerts: to show an emergency or an incoming call over the lock screen. If you don't allow it, they appear as normal notifications.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask you to create an account or sign in.
  • It does not read your contacts, call log, messages or files.
  • It does not show ads and does not use analytics or advertising SDKs.
  • It does not sell or share personal information with anyone outside your group.

Security

Messages are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from the group name and password, and each message carries its send time so an old recorded message cannot be replayed. Connections to Talkie's server use TLS. Anyone who knows the group name and password can join the group, so choose a long password and share it only with your team. Messages marked "private" are encrypted with the same group key: they are only shown to the person you send them to, but another member of the same group with a modified app could read them.

Children

The app is a work tool for teams. It is not directed at children.

Deleting your information

  • On your phone: delete the app, or remove a group in the app, to delete what the app keeps on the phone. Messages already delivered to other phones stay in their History until they delete them, or until 48 hours pass for voice messages and 90 days for texts and photos.
  • On Talkie's server: in the app, open Settings > Remove this device from Talkie's server. The device registration (and any name and phone details shared with it) is deleted at once. You can also email sales@talkiefiber.com with your app name to ask us to delete it, or simply uninstall the app: an unused registration is deleted automatically after 60 days.
